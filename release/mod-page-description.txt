A driving range anywhere in Los Santos. You stand where you are and hit ball after ball into the traffic. No hole, no course, no walking after the ball.

<b>Free.</b> Open source — the code and every release are on <a href="https://github.com/defthrets/StreetGolf">GitHub</a>.<br><br>

<b>THE GAME'S OWN GOLF, TAKEN OUTSIDE</b>

It is built on the golf minigame that is already in the game: the same swing animations, the same four club props in his hand, the same golf ball flown by the game's own physics, the same sound off the tee and the same divot. Nothing is faked. He just does it on a pavement.

You aim by looking. Point the camera at whatever you want to hit, hold the trigger and the meter climbs, then falls back, so you time it. Let go inside the green band near the top and it goes where you pointed it, with a little extra; miss the band and it hooks or slices by how far you missed. The left stick fine-tunes the line if the camera is not quite there.

A fresh ball is teed up after every shot, so you can rapid-fire at whatever drives past. Up to ten stay in the world at once.

<b>The camera stays on the ball.</b> After the strike it follows it — over the roof, off the bus, down the road — and it <i>keeps</i> following after the ball has stopped, until you press the button. Then you are back on the tee.

<b>You can lean on it while it is out.</b> Left stick in the air bends the shot sideways or holds it up; less authority once it is bouncing, less again once it is rolling, and each shot gets a fixed allowance, so it is a nudge rather than a remote control. A bar under the carry readout shows how much is left.


<b>TWELVE CLUBS</b>

Three sets of four, on the shoulder buttons.

<ul>
<li> <b>Driver, Iron, Wedge, Putter.</b> The four the minigame has, at their own launch angles and speeds.</li>
<li> <b>Driver 2, Iron 2, Wedge 2, Power Putter.</b> Flatter and a little further, so they run out along a street instead of arcing over it.</li>
<li> <b>Driver 3, Iron 3, Wedge 3, Putter 3.</b> The standard arcs, swung a great deal harder.</li>
</ul>

Every one borrows its base club's prop, animation and impact timing, so the extra sets cost nothing but a launch angle and a speed — and both are in the ini if you want your own.


<b>WHAT IT DOES TO WHAT IT HITS</b>

Everything scales with how fast the ball is going when it connects, so a putt rolls off harmlessly and a full driver does real harm.

<ul>
<li> <b>Cars take it exactly where the ball lands.</b> The panel caves in at the point of contact, body health goes with it, the alarm goes off and it rocks on its springs. A hard one above the waistline puts a window out — the right window, worked out from where on the body it struck — and a low one on a corner takes the tyre off the rim. Straight through the bonnet hurts the engine.</li>
<li> <b>Walls, roads and glass take the game's own impact damage.</b> Chipped concrete, cracked render, spidered glass and a puff of dust: the marks the game already knows how to leave.</li>
<li> <b>Pedestrians go down.</b> FORE! They ragdoll, take damage that scales with the strike, and are thrown along the ball's line.</li>
<li> <b>Loose props get shoved.</b> Bins, cones, signs.</li>
</ul>

Contact is found properly. A ball at sixty metres a second crosses a wall between two frames, so instead of trusting the collision flag a probe is swept along the path it covered since the last frame and pushed <i>past</i> where it is now — because physics halts the ball a fraction short of what it hits, and a probe drawn only between two frame positions runs entirely outside every wall. That is why it registers on a lamp post and on a window pane.


<b>FOUR BALLS</b>

<ul>
<li> <b>Normal.</b> A golf ball.</li>
<li> <b>Fireball.</b> Sets light to whatever it touches and leaves fires burning where it bounced.</li>
<li> <b>Boom.</b> Flies exactly like a normal ball, then detonates where it lands. Not before.</li>
<li> <b>Super Shot.</b> Every club multiplied. Fifty times, by default. It is not subtle.</li>
</ul>

One press in the settings drawer, or <b>M</b>.


<b>A HUD BUILT FOR IT</b>

<ul>
<li> <b>A range card</b> top left: the club in hand with its carry and a rail of the twelve showing which one and which set, the ball, the session — balls, cars, people, your best drive and your last — and the police.</li>
<li> <b>A tee marker</b> under his feet with the power meter, right where you are looking when you swing. Amber on the way up, green in the sweet spot, red past it, and it flares when the club connects.</li>
<li> <b>A feed</b> on the right for what the ball just did — SMASH!, FORE!, LONGEST DRIVE, BOOM — each with its own picture.</li>
<li> <b>The carry</b> top centre while the camera is on the ball, in the game's own big font.</li>
<li> <b>A settings drawer</b> in the card that slides open when you touch the D-pad and closes a few seconds after you stop. Ten settings, switches drawn as switches, and a line under the list saying what the selected one does.</li>
</ul>

Thirty icons drawn for it, and the button prompts along the bottom use the game's own button pictures, so they show the right buttons for a pad or a keyboard without being told. It is sized off screen height, so it does not stretch on an ultrawide.


<b>THE POLICE</b>

Handled so you can actually play.

<ul>
<li> <b>Three minutes of grace.</b> For the first three minutes of a session they ignore you completely. A clock on the card counts it down.</li>
<li> <b>Then they still do not care</b> about a man hitting golf balls, until you drop three people inside forty-five seconds. Older hits are forgotten, so the occasional casualty never builds up.</li>
<li> <b>Unseen is unseen.</b> If nobody has a line of sight to you there is no heat, regardless. The card says NO WITNESS when that is what is saving you.</li>
<li> <b>Batons and tasers.</b> A man with a golf club is a nuisance, not a gunfight. At one and two stars they come with a nightstick close in and a taser further back — unless you have drawn a firearm yourself, in which case they answer whatever you are holding. Their guns are only holstered, never taken, so they are their normal selves the moment it stops applying.</li>
</ul>

Any stars you had when you started are left alone: this stops the mod <i>creating</i> heat, it does not wipe heat you earned. One switch in the drawer, or <b>K</b>, turns the lot off if you would rather be shot at.


<b>YOURS TO CHANGE</b>

Ten settings in the drawer while you play: ball, police, batons, impact power, car damage, wall marks, trail, aim line, after-touch and units. The other sixty-odd are in StreetGolf.ini with a line each: every club's speed and loft, the sweet spot, how the trail looks, the police timings, the after-touch allowances, how hard a car gets shoved. Your best drive and your totals persist between sessions in a text file beside it.


<b>INSTALL</b>

Drag into your GTA V folder, say yes to merging, load a save, press <b>F3</b>. Needs ScriptHookV and ScriptHookVDotNet 3 — tested on 3.6, the 3.7 nightlies and 3.9 Enhanced, and it works on Legacy and Enhanced from the same files. No game file is modified and nothing is written to your save.

It is source, not a DLL: ScriptHookVDotNet compiles StreetGolf.cs when the game starts, so there is nothing to unblock, and <b>Insert</b> reloads it without restarting.

<b>❗ If you have both Legacy and Enhanced installed</b> they are separate folders with separate scripts folders. Put it in the one you actually launch.


<b>KEYS</b>

<ul>
<li><b>F3</b> — Street Golf on and off</li>
<li><b>Space</b> — hold to swing, release to hit; while the ball is out, the next ball. <b>Left mouse</b> swings too</li>
<li><b>Q</b> and <b>E</b>, or the mouse wheel — club</li>
<li><b>A</b> and <b>D</b> — fine aim; <b>W A S D</b> steers the ball in flight</li>
<li><b>Arrow keys</b> — the settings drawer</li>
<li><b>M</b> — ball; <b>K</b> — police; <b>N</b> — fresh ball; <b>Backspace</b> — quit</li>
</ul>

The F-key and the letters can be changed in the ini.

<b>On a controller:</b> hold <b>LT</b> and press <b>A</b> to start. <b>RT</b> swings, <b>LB</b> and <b>RB</b> change club, the left stick fine-aims and then steers the ball, <b>A</b> stops watching and tees the next one, the <b>D-pad</b> works the drawer and <b>B</b> quits. The prompts along the bottom switch to the pad's buttons the moment you pick it up.

<b>1.1.0</b>

<ul>
<li> <b>A new HUD, top to bottom.</b> A range card with the club, the ball, the police and the session; a tee marker under the golfer with the power meter; a feed on the right for every smash and FORE!; the carry top centre while the camera is on the ball; and a settings drawer that slides open on the D-pad. </ul>

<b>1.0.0</b>

First release.

<ul>
<li>The game's own golf, playable anywhere: its animations, clubs, ball, sounds and particles. Aim with the camera, a timed power meter with a sweet spot, a fresh ball after every shot, the camera on the ball until you say otherwise.</li>
<li>Twelve clubs in three sets. Four balls: Normal, Fireball, Boom and Super Shot.</li>
<li>Impacts found by a swept probe: cars dented where the ball lands, glass out, tyres off, walls marked, pedestrians down.</li>

</ul>
